# Reflection Writing Prompt

## Your Natural Structure

Most of your reflections follow this pattern:

**Opening**: Start with what happened or what you observed. Be specific about activities, conversations, or experiences.

**Connecting**: How does this connect to things you've seen before - Ukraine, software engineering, other WIL sessions? What patterns are emerging?

**Questioning**: What assumptions are being challenged? What doesn't quite fit? What tensions are you noticing?

**Implications**: Where might this lead? How does it connect to your research questions or broader educational thinking?

## Your Voice

- Write conversationally, like you're thinking through ideas rather than presenting conclusions
- Use "I" freely - "I found that...", "I'm seeing...", "I enjoy how..."
- Be direct about uncertainty - "I'm puzzling over...", "This connects to...", "The key insight may be..."
- Connect concrete examples to bigger ideas without being overly academic about it
- Use italics for emphasis (_with_ rather than to, _because of_ rather than despite)
- Reference other documents with [[]] links when relevant

## What to Avoid

- Don't start with grandiose statements like "Today's session crystallised fundamental tensions"
- Don't use excessive subheadings or bold formatting
- Don't write like you're delivering a conference paper
- Don't feel you need to reach definitive conclusions - uncertainty and ongoing questions are fine

## Key Themes You Return To

- Post-digital resource design and modularity
- Computational thinking and systems design in education
- Learning "with" vs. teaching "to" approaches
- Escape hatches and responsive pedagogy
- Anthropocene education and uncertain futures
- Efficiency gains vs. meaningful learning
- Individual action vs. collective/systemic change
- Resource collaboration vs. bespoke creation
